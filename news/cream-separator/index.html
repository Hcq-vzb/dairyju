Cream Separator

Published 2026-10-22 · KIWL Machine

Specifying a cream separator for dairy and juice processors is an exercise in matching three things: the rate you need, the product you run and the conditions you run it in. This guide covers each of the three, and ends with the checks to complete before you sign off.

What This Equipment Does

Every machine on a line should have one sentence that explains why it exists. For this cream separator it is: it processes 250–500 ml product at 7214–10214 units per hour, consistently, without the dairy and juice processors team chasing it around.

Key Technical Specifications

Ask what a supplier is not willing to write down. The answer, on a cream separator, is usually the tolerance at the low end of the range: this build holds 2.0 ml at 7214 units per hour, not only at 10214.

Integration With Your Line

A cream separator fits an existing dairy and juice processors production line when three things align: the transfer pitch, the control protocol and the cleaning route. This build accepts a standard encoder interface and works across a 1600 mm working width.

Capacity Planning and ROI

Include the service contract in the ROI. A cream separator with a 4-inch HMI and a 15-minute changeover loses far less output when a fault occurs, because the diagnosis is quicker and the reset is shorter.

Maintenance and Service

The wear items on this cream separator are the seals, the drive and the guide rails. None of them should fail without warning, and none of them should require a week of lead time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the cream separator behave if the upstream stops?

The buffer upstream of the cream separator determines the recovery. The machine itself restarts into the stored recipe once the upstream signal returns.

Does the cream separator need a special foundation?

No special foundation beyond a level floor with the clearance shown on the 1600 × 2200 × 2100 mm GA drawing.

How do I size this for my plant?

Base capacity on peak hourly demand plus 15–20% headroom, and confirm utility availability before ordering.

Does it integrate with existing lines?

Yes, with standard PLC interfaces and shared CIP or control logic when specified at quotation.

What is typical lead time and service?

Standard machines ship in weeks; remote commissioning and spare parts support are usually included.
