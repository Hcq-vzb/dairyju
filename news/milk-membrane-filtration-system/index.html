Milk Membrane Filtration System

Published 2026-10-24 · KIWL Machine

Choosing the right milk membrane filtration system is a practical decision for dairy and juice processors. This guide covers the criteria that matter when specifying, integrating and accepting a milk membrane filtration system on a production line, with the figures a buyer should confirm in writing before placing an order.

What This Equipment Does

A milk membrane filtration system handles a defined step in the production line for dairy and juice processors. This build runs at 1883–2383 units per hour for {sz} product, and the configuration directly affects output consistency, labour and product quality.

Key Technical Specifications

Compare rated capacity, material contact parts (typically 304/316L stainless), control type (PLC vs relay), utilities demand and changeover time. The reference build for this milk membrane filtration system is 316L stainless steel with a 4-inch HMI, a 1.5–2.5 kW connected load and a {fw_a} × {fw_b} × 3200 mm footprint, and these figures set both the price and the long-term flexibility of the line.

Integration With Your Line

Plan upstream buffer, downstream transfer and shared CIP or control logic so the milk membrane filtration system does not become the bottleneck. Compatibility with existing tanks and conveyors avoids costly rework, and this build accepts a standard PLC interface for the upstream machine.

Capacity Planning and ROI

Size the milk membrane filtration system to peak demand with modest headroom, then model payback from labour savings, yield gain and reduced downtime. At 1883–2383 units per hour a typical dairy and juice processors operation recovers the investment within 12–36 months at regional scale.

Maintenance and Service

Choose suppliers offering spare parts stock, remote support and clear manuals. Preventive maintenance on seals, sensors and drives keeps availability high, and on this milk membrane filtration system the wear items are the seals, the drive and the guide rails.

Frequently Asked Questions

What source water quality is required?

The incoming water should be within the stated TDS and turbidity range. Send a water analysis and we will confirm the pretreatment design.

How often do the membranes need replacing?

Typically every 24–36 months, depending on the inlet water quality and the actual operating hours.

How do I size this for my plant?

Base capacity on peak hourly demand plus 15–20% headroom, and confirm utility availability before ordering.

Does it integrate with existing lines?

Yes, with standard PLC interfaces and shared CIP or control logic when specified at quotation.

What is typical lead time and service?

Standard machines ship in weeks; remote commissioning and spare parts support are usually included.
